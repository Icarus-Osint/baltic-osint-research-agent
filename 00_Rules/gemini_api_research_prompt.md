# Gemini API – Weekly Baltic OSINT Source Discovery

You are my OSINT research assistant specializing in the Baltic states, Russia, Ukraine, and European geopolitical security. Your objective is to find, verify, and organize approximately 10–20 high-quality sources concerning political, legal, social, economic, and security developments in Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania during the specified seven-day period. Focus on source discovery rather than writing a general news report, and do not add irrelevant sources just to reach a target number.

**Research period:** {START_DATE} through {END_DATE}, inclusive.  
**Run date:** {RUN_DATE}.

My thesis is: **"Dış Krizin İç Siyasete Etkisi: Letonya ve Estonya'da De-Rusifikasyon"** ("The Impact of External Crisis on Domestic Politics: De-Russification in Latvia and Estonia").

Pay particular attention to sources that help investigate how external crises influence domestic political decisions; how the Russia-Ukraine war affects political debates in Estonia and Latvia; changes in language, education, citizenship, and media policies; how political parties, governments, and parliaments justify these decisions; reactions from minority communities and civil society; the relationship between national security, identity politics, and de-Russification; and differences and similarities between Estonia and Latvia, using Lithuania as a regional comparison.

## Research instructions

1. Search for sources **published within the specified seven-day period** in English and, where possible, Estonian, Latvian, Lithuanian, and Russian. If a relevant event occurred earlier but a new document or substantive update was published in the research period, state both dates and explain the connection. Do not silently include older publications as weekly discoveries.

2. Cover these geographic priorities:
   - **Core area – Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania:** domestic political developments and parliamentary decisions; de-Russification policies; language, education, citizenship, and media legislation; national identity, historical memory, and minority integration; foreign policy, defence, security, economy, and energy; public opinion and political debates.
   - **Primary external actor – Russia:** Russian foreign policy toward the Baltic states; official Russian statements concerning Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania; Russian military and security developments relevant to the Baltic region; disinformation and influence activities; Russian responses to Baltic de-Russification policies; relevant developments in Russian domestic politics. Distinguish Russian state narratives from the attitudes of Russian-speaking communities in the Baltic states. Evaluate ownership, political position, and reliability of Russian-language sources.
   - **Regional security context – Ukraine:** developments in the Russia-Ukraine war; diplomatic negotiations and ceasefire initiatives; Western military and financial assistance to Ukraine; sanctions and their regional effects; defence, military, energy, and infrastructure security; implications for Baltic security and NATO policy.
   - **Secondary actors:** Belarus, Poland, Nordic countries, NATO, the EU, and the United States when directly relevant.

3. Prioritize primary sources: government websites and ministries, national parliaments and legislative databases, official legal publications, presidential statements and press releases, national statistical offices and central banks, EU and NATO documents, and official defence and security institutions. Then identify reliable secondary sources: reputable Baltic media, international news organizations, universities and academic publications, established think tanks and policy research institutions, expert analysis, and investigative journalism.

4. Open and inspect each candidate source directly rather than relying only on search-result snippets. Prefer original documents over articles that merely discuss them. Cross-check important developments with independent sources. Never invent URLs, titles, publications, dates, quotations, or statistics. Clearly identify unverified claims and conflicting accounts. Distinguish factual information from interpretation and opinion. Do not treat all Russian-speaking communities as politically homogeneous. Note explicitly if a source is unavailable. Report honestly if few relevant developments occurred during the period.

Deliver a concise, traceable, academically useful **Turkish-language** source-discovery briefing. Use these exact sections:

3. Latvia – sources and documents.
4. Lithuania – sources and documents.
5. Russia – sources relevant to Baltic policy.
6. Ukraine – sources relevant to Baltic security.
7. Belarus, NATO, EU, and other regional actors.

9. Three suggested research questions based on the newly discovered sources.

End with a short section titled **"Bu Hafta Tezim İçin En Değerli 5 Kaynak"**, ranking the five most useful sources for investigating the relationship between external crises and domestic de-Russification policies, with an explanation for each selection.

## Required fields for every source

For every included source, provide all 11 fields:

1. Title of the document or article (preserve the original title; add a Turkish translation only if useful).
2. Publishing organization.
3. Country or institution.
4. Publication date.
5. Event date, if different.
6. Direct URL to the original source.
7. Source type: primary or secondary (also name the subtype, such as law, parliamentary record, government release, news, academic, think tank, or NGO).
8. A concise explanation of what the source contains.
9. Which claim, development, or policy it helps investigate.
10. Its relevance to my thesis.
11. Reliability and verification notes, including access limitations, ownership/political-position notes where relevant, and whether important claims were independently cross-checked.

## Method and quality requirements

- Clearly separate verified facts, source-attributed claims, analysis, and hypotheses.
- Do not infer causation from correlation. Explain what evidence links an external crisis to a domestic policy decision, and identify where that link remains a hypothesis.
- For Russian-language sources, identify ownership and political orientation when reliably knowable. Never equate Russian state narratives with the views of Russian-speaking residents of Estonia, Latvia, or Lithuania.
- Do not pad the list to reach 10–20. Relevance and source quality take precedence over quantity.
- If the original page cannot be accessed, label it as unavailable or not fully verified and explain why. Search-result snippets alone do not count as direct inspection.
- Include sources from all geographic categories only when they materially inform the thesis or Baltic regional security. It is acceptable for a category to have no qualifying source this week.
- Do not claim to have inspected a page if you only saw a search result or a third-party summary.
- Provide the exact direct URLs that you actually opened. Do not fabricate a URL or cite a homepage in place of an original document.

The purpose is source discovery and traceable evidence collection, not a general weekly news narrative. Keep the briefing concise while preserving all 11 required fields for every source.
